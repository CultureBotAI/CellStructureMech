A structure composed of peptidoglycan and often chitin in addition to other materials. porous cell septum

A septum or cross wall which does not entirely span the space between two portions of cell wall and may contain a specialized central pore structure. A porous septum allows the movement of organelles and/or cytoplasm between compartments. A cross-wall cell septum in filamentous ascomycete and basidiomycete fungi that is perforated by one or more central septal pores (such as the simple septal pore of Neurospora crassa and Aspergillus nidulans or the barrel-shaped dolipore septum of basidiomycetes). The porous cell septum partitions hyphal compartments structurally while permitting regulated intercellular cytoplasmic streaming, organelle migration, and Woronin-body- or parenthesome-mediated pore gating upon hyphal wounding.

S-layer

A paracrystalline monomolecular array of one or more (glyco)protein species that self-assemble on the outermost surface of the cell envelope, with oblique, square or hexagonal lattice symmetry. S-layers are common in Archaea, where they are often the sole wall component, and widespread but patchily distributed in Bacteria.
