cell envelope Sec protein transport complex

A transmembrane protein complex involved in the translocation of proteins across the cytoplasmic membrane. In Gram-negative bacteria, Sec-translocated proteins are subsequently secreted via the type II, IV, or V secretion systems. Sec complex components include SecA, D, E, F, G, Y and YajC. The bacterial and archaeal cell envelope general secretory (Sec) holotranslocon machinery that mediates co-translational insertion of inner membrane proteins and post-translational translocation of unfolded preproteins across the cytoplasmic membrane. Centered on the heterotrimeric SecYEG protein-conducting channel, the cell envelope Sec protein transport complex associates with the cytoplasmic SecA ATPase motor, the SecDF-YajC proton-motive-force-driven accessory complex, and the YidC membrane insertase.

The injectisome: a syringe-like envelope-spanning machine that delivers effector proteins from the bacterial cytoplasm directly into a eukaryotic host cell. type V protein secretion system complex

A complex of proteins that permits the translocation of proteins across the outer membrane via a transmembrane pore, formed by a beta-barrel, into the extracellular milieu or directly into host cells; the secreted proteins contain all the information required for translocation of an effector molecule through the cell envelope. The type V secretion systems includes the autotransporters (type Va), the two-partner secretion system (type Vb) and the Oca family (type Vc).
