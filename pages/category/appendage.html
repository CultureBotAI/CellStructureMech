9+2 motile cilium

A motile cilium where the axoneme has a ring of nine outer microtubule doublets plus two central microtubules (and is therefore called a 9+2 axoneme). A canonical motile cilium (or eukaryotic flagellum) whose axoneme consists of nine peripheral microtubule doublets surrounding a central pair of singlet microtubules (the 9+2 arrangement). Conserved across Chlamydomonas reinhardtii, Tetrahymena thermophila, and Trypanosoma brucei, the 9+2 motile cilium integrates central pair projections, radial spokes, nexin-dynein regulatory complexes, and dynein arms to produce planar or ciliary waveforms.

bacterial-type flagellum

The rotary motility organelle of bacteria: a long helical filament of flagellin joined by a flexible hook to a basal body that spans the cell envelope. The basal body houses a rotor (MS and C rings) driven by membrane-embedded stator units that convert ion flow (H+ or Na+) into torque; the flagellar type III export apparatus at its base secretes the axial subunits. Not homologous to the archaeal archaellum or the eukaryotic cilium.

ciliary base

Area of the cilium (also called flagellum) where the basal body and the axoneme are anchored to the plasma membrane. The ciliary base encompasses the distal part of the basal body, transition fibers and transition zone and is structurally and functionally very distinct from the rest of the cilium. In this area proteins are sorted and filtered before entering the cilium, and many ciliary proteins localize specifically to this area. The specialized proximal region of a eukaryotic cilium or flagellum encompassing the basal body docking site, transition fibers, and ciliary transition zone in ciliated protists and microalgae. The ciliary base acts as a molecular gate and assembly hub where intraflagellar transport (IFT) trains are loaded and membrane diffusion between the plasma membrane and ciliary membrane is regulated.

longitudinal flagellum

A motile cilium found in dinoflagellates. It trails the cell and acts as a steering rudder. It is often partially contained in a furrow called the sulcus, and emerges from a flagellar pore located in the sulcus. The posteriorly directed eukaryotic flagellum of dinoflagellates that emerges from the ventral flagellar pore, lies along the longitudinal sulcus furrow, and trails behind the cell body. Together with the transverse flagellum, the longitudinal flagellum beats with planar waves to provide directional steering and forward propulsion in free-living and symbiotic dinoflagellates.

motile cilium

A cilium which may have a variable arrangement of axonemal microtubules and also contains molecular motors. It may beat with a whip-like pattern that promotes cell motility or transport of fluids and other cells across a cell surface, such as on epithelial cells that line the lumenal ducts of various tissues; or they may display a distinct twirling motion that directs fluid flow asymmetrically across the cellular surface to affect asymmetric body plan organization. Motile cilia can be found in single as well as multiple copies per cell. A membrane-bounded ciliary projection capable of active whip-like or undulating beating that propels swimming cells or generates fluid flow across microbial cell surfaces. In flagellated green algae (Chlamydomonas reinhardtii), ciliates (Tetrahymena thermophila), and kinetoplastids (Trypanosoma brucei), the motile cilium contains an axoneme powered by outer and inner dynein arms and regulated by radial spokes and intraflagellar transport (IFT) trains.
