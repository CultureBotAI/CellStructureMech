ascospore-type prospore

An immature spore undergoing development. The spore usually consists of nucleic acid, prospore membrane(s) that encase the nucleic acid, and ultimately a cell wall that covers the membrane(s). This type of spore is observed in ascospore-forming fungi. The nascent intracellular spore compartment formed during meiosis II in ascomycete yeasts such as Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Schizosaccharomyces pombe before spore wall maturation. Each ascospore-type prospore is delimited by the double-layered prospore membrane that grows from the spindle pole body to engulf a haploid nucleus and maternal cytoplasm.

ascospore-type prospore nucleus

Any nucleus that is part of a ascospore-type prospore. In sporulating Saccharomyces cerevisiae and Schizosaccharomyces pombe asci, each of the four haploid nuclear lobes generated by meiosis II is engulfed by an expanding prospore membrane nucleated at the meiotic outer plaque of the spindle pole body, partitioning one haploid ascospore-type prospore nucleus into each developing prospore.

The layered envelope that surrounds a bacterial endospore and gives it its resistance: a thick modified peptidoglycan cortex, a proteinaceous coat of dozens of proteins outside it, and in some species an additional loose exosporium.
