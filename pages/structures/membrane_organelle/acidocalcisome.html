acidocalcisome

GO:0020022 · Membrane organelle · Membrane bounded organelle · Proposed

An acidic electron-dense membrane-bounded organelle found in diverse microbes that stores polyphosphate, pyrophosphate, calcium and other cations and uses proton pumps and cation transporters to maintain its ion-rich matrix. [GO:0020022]

Synonyms: metachromatic granule; volutin granule; polyphosphate vacuole

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — VARIABLE: Acidocalcisome-like compartments have been reported in Agrobacterium tumefaciens and Rhodospirillum rubrum. The bacterial identification is disputed: Frank and Jendrossek 2020 found distinct polyphosphate granules and HppA-associated structures in C58 by fluorescence and mutant analysis, without TEM confirmation; Docampo 2024 disputes that interpretation. These observations do not establish universal identity of bacterial polyphosphate granules with membrane-bounded acidocalcisomes. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1097]
  • NCBITaxon:2759 Eukaryota — VARIABLE: Acidocalcisomes are widespread in diverse microbial eukaryotes and metazoan cell types but are not universal across all eukaryotes. [DOI:10.1128/MMBR.00042-23]

Mechanism graphs

Proton pumps and polyphosphate build an acidic cation store (FUNCTION)

The acidocalcisome membrane bounds a polyphosphate-rich matrix. Proton pumps acidify the lumen, and the anionic polyphosphate matrix chelates calcium and other cations to create an electron-dense storage compartment.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): Resolve exact bacterial and eukaryotic acidocalcisome proton-pump and cation-transporter families rather than grouping all acidification and ion-transport proteins together. The first record captures the transport roles required to maintain an acidic cation store, but V-H+-PPase, V-H+-ATPase and calcium transporter complements vary by lineage.
  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): How do bacterial polyphosphate granules relate to acidocalcisome-like membrane structures? Seufferheld et al. 2003 reported membrane-bounded, acidic, polyphosphate- and cation-rich Agrobacterium granules using microscopy, localization and fractionation. Frank and Jendrossek 2020 instead separated polyphosphate granules from HppA-associated foci in C58 using fluorescence and mutants; they did not perform TEM. Docampo 2024 counters that growth phase, fluorescent tags and detection of short polyphosphate chains limit the contrary interpretation. The disagreement remains unresolved and does not justify declaring bacterial acidocalcisomes absent or all bacterial polyphosphate granules equivalent to this organelle. Original 2003 strain context still requires direct verification.
