Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:5794 Apicomplexa — VARIABLE: The apicoplast is present in many apicomplexan parasites, including Plasmodium and Toxoplasma, but has been lost in some lineages. [DOI:10.1016/j.ijpara.2016.08.005]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:5833 Plasmodium falciparum — Human malaria parasite in which IPP rescue showed that apicoplast isoprenoid precursor biosynthesis is essential in blood-stage growth. [DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001138]
  • NCBITaxon:5811 Toxoplasma gondii — Apicomplexan model used to show that Tic20 is required for protein import into the plastid. [DOI:10.1073/pnas.0803862105]

Functions

  • nuclear-encoded protein import — Most apicoplast proteins are encoded in the nucleus and imported across the surrounding membranes using bipartite targeting signals and membrane translocons.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.95.21.12352 Waller et al. 1998 demonstrated targeting of nuclear-encoded proteins to the plastid in Toxoplasma gondii and Plasmodium falciparum.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.0803862105 van Dooren et al. 2008 showed that Toxoplasma Tic20 is essential for apicoplast protein import.
  • isoprenoid precursor biosynthesis — The Plasmodium falciparum apicoplast makes the isoprenoid precursor IPP during blood-stage parasite growth.
    • DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.1001138 Yeh and DeRisi 2011 showed that exogenous IPP rescues blood-stage P. falciparum lacking apicoplast function.

Mechanism graphs

Bipartite leaders route nuclear-encoded cargo into the apicoplast (FUNCTION)

Nuclear-encoded apicoplast proteins carry a bipartite leader that directs them into the secretory pathway and then through apicoplast membrane translocons, including SELMA/ERAD-derived machinery and the Tic20 inner-membrane translocon.
