toxicyst

cellstructuremech:toxicyst · MEMBRANE_ORGANELLE · MEMBRANE_BOUNDED_ORGANELLE · PROPOSED

A ciliate offensive extrusome that stores toxic discharge material and ejects on prey contact to immobilize prey during raptorial feeding. [DOI:10.1186/s12915-024-01904-2]

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:197905 Haptoria — COMMON: Raptorial haptorian ciliates carry oral toxicysts, and long type I toxicysts are described as consistently present across Haptoria. [DOI:10.1186/s12915-024-01904-2]
  • NCBITaxon:60000 Coleps hirtus — COMMON: Coleps hirtus cells carry toxicysts that cluster mainly in and around the oral aperture and assist carnivorous feeding. [DOI:10.1111/jeu.12106]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:60000 Coleps hirtus — Freshwater ciliate in which toxicyst discharge material was isolated from living cells and tested for cytotoxic effects against other ciliates. [DOI:10.1111/jeu.12106]

Functions

  • prey immobilization — Toxicysts rapidly discharge during ciliate predation and release toxic material that helps immobilize prey.
    • DOI:10.1111/jeu.12106 Buonanno et al. 2014 tested the cytotoxic effects of Coleps hirtus toxicyst discharge and connected it to effective predatory behavior.
    • DOI:10.1186/s12915-024-01904-2 Chen et al. 2024 observed raptorial haptorian ciliates using toxicysts near the oral apparatus during predation.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): Resolve exact small-molecule and biosynthetic boundaries for toxicyst toxic discharge material. Coleps discharge contains several free fatty acids and a minor amount of phytanic acid, while haptorian genomes are enriched for candidate toxin-related PKS and L-amino acid oxidase genes; this first record therefore leaves the toxic material aggregated until exact secreted molecules are localized to toxicysts.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Do not curate haptorian PKS or L-amino acid oxidase expansions as mature toxicyst components without direct toxicyst localization. Chen et al. 2024 identified PKS and L-amino acid oxidase genes as duplicated candidate toxin genes in Haptoria, but those genes were not resolved as mature structural cargo or membrane constituents of the toxicyst organelle.
