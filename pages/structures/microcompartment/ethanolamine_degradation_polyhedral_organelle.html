ethanolamine degradation polyhedral organelle

GO:0031471 · MICROCOMPARTMENT · PROTEIN_SHELLED_ORGANELLE · PROPOSED

An organelle found in bacteria consisting of a proteinaceous coat containing enzymes for the degradation of ethanolamine whose purpose is the protection of the rest of the cell from the toxic acetaldehyde product of the enzyme ethanolamine ammonia lyase. [GO:0031471]

Synonyms: Eut metabolosome; ethanolamine utilization bacterial microcompartment

Functions

  • ethanolamine catabolic process GO:0046336 — Sequesters ethanolamine-catabolic chemistry in a protein shell so the cell can convert ethanolamine through acetaldehyde to acetyl-phosphate, acetyl-CoA, and ethanol.
    • DOI:10.1128/JB.02179-12 Huseby and Roth used Salmonella genetics to test how the Eut microcompartment routes ethanolamine catabolism and recycles private NAD and CoA pools.
  • acetaldehyde and cofactor confinement — Restricts acetaldehyde and large NAD/CoA cofactors from freely diffusing between the lumen and cytosol during ethanolamine breakdown.
    • DOI:10.1128/JB.02179-12 Genetic evidence supported retention and recycling of NAD and CoA within the Eut compartment and restricted diffusion of the acetaldehyde intermediate.
