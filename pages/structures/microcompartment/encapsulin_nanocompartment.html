encapsulin nanocompartment

GO:0140737 · MICROCOMPARTMENT · PROTEIN_SHELLED_ORGANELLE · PROPOSED

A proteinaceous nanocompartment, 24 to 42 nm across, built from a single HK97-fold shell protein that self-assembles into an icosahedron. Cargo enzymes are packaged inside by a short targeting peptide that binds the shell interior; the commonest cargoes are ferritin-like iron-mineralising proteins and peroxidases. [GO:0140737]

Synonyms: protein nanocompartment

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:1773 Mycobacterium tuberculosis — Source of the first characterised encapsulin, the 'Cfp29' shell with a DyP cargo. [DOI:10.1016/j.cbpa.2016.05.013]

Functions

  • iron ion sequestration — Ferritin-like cargo mineralises iron inside the shell, buffering the cell against iron and oxidative stress.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.cbpa.2016.05.013 Giessen 2016 reviews iron mineralisation as the commonest encapsulin function.
  • enzyme compartmentalisation — Confines a reaction and its reactive intermediates away from the cytoplasm.
    • DOI:10.1038/nmicrobiol.2017.29 The 2017 survey frames encapsulins as a widespread compartmentalisation strategy.
