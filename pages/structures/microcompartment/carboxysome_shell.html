carboxysome shell

cellstructuremech:carboxysome_shell · MICROCOMPARTMENT · MULTIPROTEIN_COMPLEX · PROPOSED

The polyhedral protein coat of alpha- and beta-carboxysomes, built from BMC-H, BMC-T, and BMC-P shell proteins that enclose RuBisCO and carbonic anhydrase while controlling metabolite traffic between the lumen and cytosol. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro.2018.10]

Synonyms: carboxysome protein shell; alpha-carboxysome shell; beta-carboxysome shell

Part of: GO:0031470

Functions

  • selective metabolite permeability — Pores through shell facets allow small metabolites such as bicarbonate to cross the shell while restricting larger or unfavorably charged solutes.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro.2018.10 Kerfeld et al. 2018 review selective metabolite flux through pores in BMC-H and BMC-T carboxysome shell proteins.
  • CO2 retention — The shell acts as a CO2 leakage barrier, retaining carbon dioxide generated inside the lumen for fixation by encapsulated RuBisCO.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro.2018.10 Kerfeld et al. 2018 describe the carboxysome shell as limiting CO2 leakage from the compartment.
    • DOI:10.1371/journal.pone.0007521 Cai et al. 2009 experimentally linked CsoS4 vertex caps to closed shells that act as a CO2 leakage barrier in Halothiobacillus neapolitanus.

Mechanism graphs

Shell tiles close a selective carboxysome barrier (FUNCTION)

BMC-H, BMC-T, and BMC-P paralogues build a closed polyhedral carboxysome shell. Pores in the shell allow bicarbonate entry while the shell slows CO2 escape from the lumen.
