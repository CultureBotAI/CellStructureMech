elongasome

cellstructuremech:elongasome · DIVISION_MACHINERY · MULTIPROTEIN_COMPLEX · PROPOSED

The sidewall peptidoglycan-synthesis machine of rod-shaped bacteria, also called the Rod complex. A SEDS-family glycosyltransferase (RodA) works with a class B penicillin-binding protein (PBP2) to polymerise and crosslink new wall material into the cylinder, while MreC, MreD and RodZ couple the complex to MreB filaments, which distribute it around the circumference. It is the elongation counterpart of the divisome. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro2677]

Synonyms: Rod complex; RodA-PBP2 complex

Part of: GO:0009274

Mechanism graphs

Peptidoglycan synthesis moves the complex, and the sidewall extends without widening (FUNCTION)

The cited sources put the motor in the synthase, not the filament. Inhibiting the elongation-specific transpeptidase stops filament movement, so peptidoglycan synthesis drives the motion rather than being carried by it. MreB and RodZ set where and in which direction that synthesis happens; losing either gives a round cell.

Discussions

  • KNOWLEDGE_GAP (OPEN): This machine is guided by the MreB filament, and the schema has no relation that says so. The partner statement to the discussion on the MreB filament record (#119). RodZ couples the two, which is recorded in that component's role, but the structure-to-structure relationship has no field.
  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): definition_source cites Typas et al. 2011 for a definition that names RodA as a SEDS-family glycosyltransferase, but RodA was shown to be the polymerase in 2016 and the string RodA does not occur in that review. Should the definition be reworded to what Typas supports, or the definition_source changed to Meeske et al. 2016 (DOI:10.1038/nature19331)? The RodA component now carries Meeske 2016 with verbatim support (#161), so the claim itself is sourced.
