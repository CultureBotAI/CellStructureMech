starch utilization system complex

GO:0044570 · Other · Multiprotein complex · Proposed

A bacterial cell envelope-associated multiprotein system, which binds and degrades starch. [GO:0044570]

Synonyms: Sus complex

Part of: cell outer membrane (GO:0009279)

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:818 Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron — B. thetaiotaomicron is the model organism in which SusC, SusD, SusE, SusF, and SusG were characterized as outer-membrane starch utilization proteins. [DOI:10.1128/JB.182.19.5365-5372.2000]

Functions

  • cell-surface starch binding and degradation — Binds starch at the bacterial cell surface, positions it on SusC/D/E/F outer-membrane proteins, and hydrolyzes starch through the SusG surface alpha-amylase to initiate starch import.
    • DOI:10.1128/JB.182.19.5365-5372.2000 Shipman et al. 2000 showed that SusC, SusD, and SusE cooperate in starch binding and that SusF is surface exposed in the B. thetaiotaomicron Sus system.
    • DOI:10.1128/JB.181.23.7206-7211.1999 Shipman et al. 1999 established SusG as the Sus outer-membrane protein responsible for cell-surface starch hydrolysis.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): Resolve exact source-neutral family groundings for SusC, SusD, SusE, SusF, and SusG in the starch utilization system complex. The initial record uses reviewed gene-family labels from B. thetaiotaomicron literature and avoids guessing InterPro, Pfam, NCBIfam, or UniProtKB identifiers that could broaden a Sus starch-system component to unrelated TonB transporters, SusD-like proteins, or alpha-amylases.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Should SusA, SusB, or SusR be curated as constituents of the outer-membrane starch utilization system complex? Martens et al. 2009 place SusA and SusB in the periplasm and describe SusR as an inner-membrane regulator, whereas the curated SusCDEFG constituents are outer-membrane proteins. SusA, SusB, and SusR are therefore out of scope for this outer-membrane complex.

Evidence

  • GO:0044570 GO:0044570 is an active cellular-component term for the starch utilization system complex, carries the exact synonym Sus complex, and defines the complex as a bacterial cell-envelope-associated multiprotein system that binds and degrades starch.
  • DOI:10.1074/jbc.R109.022848 Martens, Koropatkin, Smith and Gordon 2009 review the Bacteroidetes Sus-like paradigm and the B. thetaiotaomicron starch utilization system.
  • DOI:10.1128/JB.182.19.5365-5372.2000 Shipman, Berleman and Salyers 2000 characterized SusC, SusD, SusE, and SusF as outer-membrane proteins involved in binding starch to the B. thetaiotaomicron cell surface.
  • DOI:10.1128/JB.181.23.7206-7211.1999 Shipman, Cho, Siegel and Salyers 1999 physiologically characterized SusG as the outer-membrane starch-hydrolyzing component of the Sus system.
