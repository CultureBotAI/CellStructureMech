apical complex

GO:0020007 · OTHER · OTHER · PROPOSED

A group of cytoskeletal structures and associated membrane-bounded organelles at the anterior end of apicomplexan parasites that supports host-cell attachment, penetration, and parasite proliferation. [GO:0020007]

Functions

  • host-cell invasion — The apical complex positions cytoskeletal and secretory parts that support attachment to and invasion of host cells by apicomplexan zoites.
    • DOI:10.1371/journal.ppat.0020013 Hu et al. 2006 studied the Toxoplasma apical complex as a cytoskeletal invasion machine.
    • DOI:10.1128/mmbr.00197-22 Waller and Carruthers 2024 review apical-complex and secretory-organelle diversity in Myzozoa and Apicomplexa.
  • ordered apical-organelle secretion — Micronemes and rhoptries at the apical complex discharge in an ordered sequence during invasion, coordinating attachment, moving-junction formation and vacuole establishment.
    • PMID:9208224 Carruthers and Sibley 1997 showed that Toxoplasma micronemes, rhoptries and dense granules secrete sequentially during invasion.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1800 Boothroyd and Dubremetz 2008 review rhoptry secretion after host-cell attachment and at the start of invasion.
