CetZ filament

cellstructuremech:cetz_filament · CYTOSKELETON · FILAMENT · PROPOSED

An archaeal tubulin-like cytoskeletal filament assembled from CetZ proteins that forms dynamic envelope-associated structures and supports cell-shape remodeling such as rod development in Haloferax volcanii. [DOI:10.1038/nature13983]

  • DOI:10.1038/nature13983 Duggin et al. 2015 showed that Haloferax volcanii CetZ1 forms dynamic cytoskeletal structures in vivo and that CetZ crystal structures adopt the FtsZ/tubulin superfamily fold.
  • DOI:10.3390/biom13010134 Brown and Duggin 2023 reviewed the diversity and potential multifunctionality of archaeal CetZ tubulin-like cytoskeletal proteins.

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2157 Archaea — VARIABLE: CetZ tubulin-like cytoskeletal proteins are archaeal FtsZ/tubulin-superfamily proteins and are not asserted to occur in every archaeal lineage. [DOI:10.3390/biom13010134]
  • NCBITaxon:2246 Haloferax volcanii — COMMON: Haloferax volcanii is the model haloarchaeon in which CetZ1 dynamic filaments and CetZ-dependent rod-cell development were experimentally characterized. [DOI:10.1038/nature13983]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:2246 Haloferax volcanii — Pleomorphic haloarchaeon used to connect CetZ1 dynamic cytoskeletal structures to envelope remodeling, rod-shape development and efficient swimming motility. [DOI:10.1038/nature13983]

Functions

  • Haloferax rod cell development — CetZ1-containing filaments remodel the Haloferax volcanii cell envelope to promote the transition from irregular plate-shaped cells into motile rod-shaped cells.
    • DOI:10.1038/nature13983 Duggin et al. 2015 showed that CetZ1 is required for rod-shaped cell development and efficient swimming motility in Haloferax volcanii.

Discussions

This first record curates CetZ1/CetZ2-supported cell-shape cytoskeletal filaments without asserting that a broad FtsZ/tubulin domain or a Haloferax-specific accession exactly denotes archaeal CetZ filament-forming proteins.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): FtsZ division proteins are paralogous tubulin-family proteins rather than CetZ filament constituents. CetZ proteins coexist with FtsZ in many archaea and the Haloferax FtsZ1 division ring is distinct from the dynamic CetZ1 structures that control rod development.
