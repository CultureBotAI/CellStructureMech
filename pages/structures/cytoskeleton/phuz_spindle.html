PhuZ spindle

cellstructuremech:phuz_spindle · CYTOSKELETON · FILAMENT · PROPOSED

A phage-encoded tubulin-like cytoskeletal spindle assembled from PhuZ polymers that positions the jumbo-phage nucleus in infected bacterial cells. [DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.07.064]

Synonyms: phage tubulin spindle

PhuZPROTEINpart ofREVIEWED_LABEL_ONLYphuZESSENTIALPhage-encoded tubulin-like protein that polymerizes into dynamic filaments of the spindle that positions jumbo-phage nuclei in infected bacterial cells.
  • DOI:10.7554/eLife.03197 Erb et al. 2014 characterized Pseudomonas phage 201phi2-1 PhuZ as a dynamically unstable bacteriophage tubulin that forms filaments to position phage DNA during infection.
  • DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.07.064 Chaikeeratisak et al. 2017 showed that dynamic PhuZ polymers form bipolar spindles associated with nucleus-like compartments in large Pseudomonas phages.

Functions

  • phage nucleus positioning — The PhuZ spindle pushes nucleus-like jumbo-phage replication compartments away from bacterial cell poles and toward midcell.
    • DOI:10.7554/eLife.03197 Erb et al. 2014 linked PhuZ dynamic instability to centering phage DNA in Pseudomonas chlororaphis cells infected by 201phi2-1.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.07.064 Chaikeeratisak et al. 2017 connected dynamic PhuZ spindles with nucleus-like compartment positioning in PhiKZ and PhiPA3 infections.
  • phage nucleus rotation — A vortex-like PhuZ cytoskeleton can rotate a mature phage nucleus during jumbo-phage replication.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2022.111179 Birkholz et al. 2022 showed that Goslar PhuZ filaments form a vortex-like cytoskeleton that drives phage nucleus rotation during Escherichia coli infection.
