PhuZ spindle

cellstructuremech:phuz_spindle · Cytoskeleton · Filament · Proposed

A phage-encoded tubulin-like cytoskeletal spindle assembled from PhuZ polymers that positions the jumbo-phage nucleus in infected bacterial cells. [DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.07.064]

Synonyms: phage tubulin spindle

Part of: GO:0005856 — cytoskeleton (no local record)

Functions

  • phage nucleus positioning — The PhuZ spindle pushes nucleus-like jumbo-phage replication compartments away from bacterial cell poles and toward midcell.
    • DOI:10.7554/eLife.03197 Erb et al. 2014 linked PhuZ dynamic instability to centering phage DNA in Pseudomonas chlororaphis cells infected by 201phi2-1.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.celrep.2017.07.064 Chaikeeratisak et al. 2017 connected dynamic PhuZ spindles with nucleus-like compartment positioning in PhiKZ and PhiPA3 infections.
  • phage nucleus rotation — The bipolar PhuZ spindle can rotate the phage nucleus during Pseudomonas phage 201phi2-1 infection, as opposing filaments act on opposite sides of the nucleus.
    • DOI:10.1126/science.aal2130 Chaikeeratisak et al. 2017 Figure 1 and time-lapse experiments show nucleus movement and rotation with dynamic PhuZ; the static D190A mutant strongly disrupts both.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): Resolve exact source-neutral grounding for phage PhuZ tubulins that build nucleus-associated cytoskeletal systems. This first record curates PhuZ proteins as phage-encoded tubulins that form nucleus-positioning spindles or nucleus-rotating vortex arrays without asserting that a broad FtsZ/tubulin-family domain or a virus-specific accession exactly denotes the full functional PhuZ family.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Chimallin and the phage nucleus are graph-scoped cargo rather than PhuZ spindle constituents. PhuZ forms the cytoskeletal spindle that positions the chimallin-lined phage nucleus; chimallin builds the separate shell and is not asserted as a constituent of the PhuZ polymer.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Keep the bipolar spindle distinct from the Goslar PhuZ cytoskeletal vortex. Earlier review issues #481 and #482 added Goslar occurrence and broadened PhuZ component wording to match the then-curated vortex rotation function. Birkholz et al. 2022 explicitly distinguish the Goslar vortex from a bipolar spindle and report that it does not center the nucleus at midcell. The existing record identifier, definition and positioning graph denote the spindle, not every PhuZ array.
