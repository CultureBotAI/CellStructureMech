conoid

GO:0020010 · Cytoskeleton · Other · Proposed

A spiral cytoskeletal structure located at the apical end of the apical complex in some apicomplexan parasites. Fibers form a left-handed spiral, and are comprised of tubulin protofilaments organized in a ribbon-like structure that differs from the conventional tubular structure characteristic of microtubules. [GO:0020010]

Part of: GO:0005856 — cytoskeleton (no local record); apical complex (GO:0020007)

Functions

  • apical cytoskeletal scaffolding — The conoid forms a spiral tubulin-based scaffold at the apex of the apical complex.
    • DOI:10.1083/jcb.200112086 Hu et al. 2002 resolved Toxoplasma conoid fibers as tubulin protofilament ribbons organized into a spiral.
  • conoid-associated host cell entry and exit — The conoid-associated MyoH motor supports Toxoplasma gliding motility, host-cell entry, and host-cell exit.
    • DOI:10.1371/journal.ppat.1005388 Graindorge et al. 2016 found that MyoH is indispensable for Toxoplasma entry and exit from host cells.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (OPEN): Resolve exact source-neutral groundings for conoid tubulin fibers, conserved conoid complex proteins, and MyoH. The first conoid record keeps Toxoplasma-localized protein cohorts label-only because no exact cross-source protein-family CURIE was curated for the unusual conoid tubulin polymer, the conserved conoid proteome, or apicomplexan MyoH.
  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Distinguish the spiral conoid body from the larger conoid-complex proteome. Hu 2006 enriched apical cytoskeletal preparations contain candidate proteins, not a pure inventory of conoid-body constituents. Koreny 2021 directly resolves distinct body, base, canopy and APR locations and documents reduced conoid-derived ring structures in Plasmodium. Homology or broad complex membership does not put every protein inside the ribbon-tubulin body or establish majority prevalence of that morphology.
