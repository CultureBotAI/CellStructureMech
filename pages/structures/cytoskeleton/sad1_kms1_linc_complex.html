Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex

GO:1990612 · Cytoskeleton · Multiprotein complex · Proposed

A LINC complex implicated in the connection of DNA double strand breaks to the cytoskeleton during DNA double-strand break repair. The canonical fission yeast (Schizosaccharomyces pombe) Linker of Nucleoskeleton and Cytoskeleton (LINC) complex spanning the inner and outer nuclear membranes, composed of the inner nuclear membrane SUN-domain protein Sad1 and the outer nuclear membrane KASH-domain protein Kms1 (and Kms2). During meiotic prophase, the Sad1–Kms1 LINC complex interacts with Bqt1–Bqt2 on the nucleoplasmic side and cytoplasmic dynein motors on the cytoplasmic side to gather telomeres to the spindle pole body and transmit microtubule forces across the nuclear envelope. [GO:1990612]

Functions

  • telomere organization GO:0032200 — Mediated by Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex (GO:1990612) within the Sad1-Kms1 LINC nuclear-envelope bridge complex. Polarized organization of chromosome ends during meiotic prophase.
    • DOI:10.26508/lsa.202000824 Direct imaging of Rap1-marked peripheral clusters.
    • DOI:10.1038/celldisc.2017.41 Taz1-marked telomeres remain clustered near the SPB during prophase.
  • Support of homolog pairing and meiotic recombination — Mediated by Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex (GO:1990612) within the Sad1-Kms1 LINC nuclear-envelope bridge complex. The budding-yeast tether/bouquet system supports efficient homolog pairing and gene conversion, without making clustering alone the exclusive cause.
    • DOI:10.26508/lsa.202000824 Mps2-depleted and csm4-deleted cells show delayed pairing at LYS4 and reduced gene conversion at ARG4/HIS4. Tethering, motion and clustering effects are not experimentally isolated from one another.
  • Support of meiotic spindle formation — Mediated by Sad1-Kms1 LINC complex (GO:1990612) within the Sad1-Kms1 LINC nuclear-envelope bridge complex. Maintained fission-yeast telomere-SPB attachment supports normal SPB behavior and spindle formation.
    • DOI:10.1038/celldisc.2017.41 Premature Bqt1 destruction increases spindle defects. Some bouquet-deficient cells still divide successfully, so no absolute requirement or universal fungal function is asserted.
