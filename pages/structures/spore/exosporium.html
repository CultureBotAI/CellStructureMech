exosporium

GO:0043592 · SPORE · ENVELOPE_LAYER · PROPOSED

The outermost layer of a bacterial endospore, which is loosely attached and located outside of the endospore coat. It is generally composed of protein, carbohydrate, and perhaps lipid. [GO:0043592]

Synonyms: epispore; exospore; perispore

Part of: GO:0005618

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:1239 Bacillota — VARIABLE: Bacterial exosporia are a patchy outer-spore layer best characterized in Bacillus cereus-group species, present in selected spore-forming Bacillota and absent from Bacillus subtilis spores. [DOI:10.1128/MMBR.00050-15]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:1392 Bacillus anthracis — Bacillus cereus-group model whose mature spores carry a loose basal-layer and nap exosporium used for exosporium assembly and surface-architecture studies. [DOI:10.1128/MMBR.00050-15]

Functions

  • outer spore surface interface — The exosporium forms the outermost surface of exosporium-bearing bacterial endospores and presents a semipermeable layer for environmental and host interactions.
    • DOI:10.1128/MMBR.00050-15 Stewart 2015 reviews the bacterial exosporium as the outer spore layer that interacts with the environment and, in pathogens, host innate-immune surfaces.
    • DOI:10.1111/mmi.13650 Terry et al. 2017 connect Bacillus cereus-group exosporium basal-layer architecture with surface exposure, semipermeability, and channels through the layer.

Discussions

  • INTERPRETATION (RESOLVED): Keep the bacterial exosporium separate from the microsporidian-type exospore and generic UniProt Perispore location. GO:0043592 explicitly denotes the bacterial endospore exosporium, whereas GO:0090642 denotes a microsporidian spore-wall exospore and UniProt SL-0367 uses Perispore generically for bacterial and eukaryotic outer spore surfaces.
