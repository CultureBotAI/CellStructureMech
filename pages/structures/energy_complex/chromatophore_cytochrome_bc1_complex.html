chromatophore cytochrome bc1 complex

cellstructuremech:chromatophore_cytochrome_bc1_complex · Energy complex · Multiprotein complex · Proposed

A purple-bacterial chromatophore membrane cytochrome bc1 protein complex that oxidizes quinol from the chromatophore quinone pool and supplies electrons to return carriers during cyclic photosynthetic electron transport. The carrier depends on the organism; cytochrome c2 is the characterized route in Cereibacter (Rhodobacter) sphaeroides. [DOI:10.1038/s41467-021-21397-9]

Synonyms: cytochrome bc1 complex

Part of: plasma membrane-derived chromatophore membrane (GO:0042717)

UniProt location names: UniProt Consortium, CC BY 4.0. Reference label and route sources.

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — RARE: Chromatophore cytochrome bc1 complexes occur in purple-bacterial photosynthetic membranes rather than broadly across bacteria. [uniprot.location:SL-0042 — Cellular chromatophore membrane]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:1063 Cereibacter sphaeroides — Purple photosynthetic bacterium, still commonly cited under the synonym Rhodobacter sphaeroides, whose chromatophore quinone traffic between reaction-center antenna cores and cytochrome bc1 has been modeled. [DOI:10.1074/jbc.M412088200]

Functions

  • photosynthetic electron transport chain GO:0009767 — Reoxidizes quinol and returns electrons toward reaction centers through organism-dependent carriers; in Cereibacter (Rhodobacter) sphaeroides this includes soluble cytochrome c2.
    • DOI:10.1074/jbc.M412088200 Comayras et al. 2005 modeled chromatophore quinone-domain behavior around cytochrome bc1 and reaction-center antenna complexes.
    • DOI:10.1038/s41467-021-21397-9 Kawakami et al. 2021, Introduction, distinguishes soluble c-type cytochromes and HiPIP return carriers and specifically identifies the bc1-to-RC cytochrome c2 route in R. sphaeroides; its primary structure is a T. tepidum HiPIP:LH1-RC complex.
