polyhydroxyalkanoate granule

GO:0070088 · INCLUSION · INCLUSION_BODY · PROPOSED

A cytoplasmic prokaryotic inclusion body in which polyhydroxyalkanoate storage polymers condense with granule-associated proteins, including PHA synthases and phasins, to form hydrophobic carbon- and reducing-power storage granules. [DOI:10.1128/JB.01723-08]

Synonyms: PHA granule; PHB granule

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — VARIABLE: PHA granules occur in phylogenetically diverse bacteria that encode PHA synthesis systems, but are absent from many other bacterial lineages. [DOI:10.1128/JB.01723-08]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:381666 Cupriavidus necator H16 — PHB-accumulating model bacterium, historically Ralstonia eutropha H16, used to localize PhaC, phasins and PhaM during PHB granule formation. [DOI:10.1128/JB.00779-12]

Functions

  • carbon and reducing-power storage — PHA granules store excess carbon and reducing equivalents as intracellular polyester inclusions that can be mobilized when external nutrients become limiting.
    • DOI:10.1128/JB.01723-08 Jendrossek 2009 reviews bacterial PHA granules as carbon and energy storage inclusions.
    • DOI:10.1128/AEM.01161-16 Mezzina and Pettinari 2016 describe the role of PHA granules as intracellular carbon and reducing-power stores.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should a phospholipid monolayer be curated as a PHA granule component? GO:0070088 still mentions a phospholipid monolayer from the older structural model, but Bresan et al. 2016 reported that purified PHA granules contain PHA and protein and no detectable phospholipids, so the monolayer is deliberately not curated as a component.
