bacterial aggresome

cellstructuremech:bacterial_aggresome · Inclusion · Inclusion body · Proposed

A stress-associated, membraneless cytoplasmic inclusion in bacteria that concentrates endogenous proteins and can sequester mRNA; Escherichia coli aggresomes exhibit liquid-like dynamics and preserve selected transcripts during stress. [DOI:10.1038/s41564-025-02086-5]

Synonyms: bacterial stress granule

Part of: GO:0005737 — cytoplasm (no local record)

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Keep bacterial aggresomes distinct from the dynein-dependent GO aggresome and RNase E-containing BR-bodies. The GO aggresome definition specifies microtubules and dynein, whereas the bacterial entity is a stress-associated protein/RNA condensate. Shared use of stress-granule terminology does not establish identity with every bacterial or eukaryotic RNA granule.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Separate observed constituents from upstream stress triggers and normally excluded enzymes. Co-localization establishes constituent membership but not assembly essentiality. HokB induces ATP depletion upstream, while wild-type RNB, RBN and ORN are normally excluded from the condensate under the tested stresses.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Avoid inferring lineage prevalence or a universal survival effect from induced cellular experiments. Presence after stress in MG1655 is an experimental example, not a measure of how consistently a lineage carries the structure. Growth recovery, dormancy and antibiotic survival are distinct outcomes affected by the perturbations used.

Evidence

  • DOI:10.1126/sciadv.abh2929 Jin et al. 2021, Membraneless organelles formed by liquid-liquid phase separation increase bacterial fitness. Results and methods read through NCBI PMC BioC (PMC8528417). Protein tracking, fusion and photobleaching support liquid-like dynamics in the assayed states.
  • DOI:10.1038/s41564-025-02086-5 Pei et al. 2025, Aggresomes protect mRNA under stress in Escherichia coli. Results, methods and Figures 5-6 read in the openly available accepted manuscript at https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/229675/ .
