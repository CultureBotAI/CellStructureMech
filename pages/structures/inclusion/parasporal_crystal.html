parasporal crystal

cellstructuremech:parasporal_crystal · INCLUSION · INCLUSION_BODY · PROPOSED

A proteinaceous Bacillus thuringiensis inclusion body that accumulates Cry, Cyt, and related pesticidal crystal proteins as a parasporal crystalline deposit. [DOI:10.3390/toxins6123296]

Synonyms: parasporal crystalline inclusion; parasporal body

Cry and Cyt pesticidal crystal proteinsPROTEINpart ofREVIEWED_LABEL_ONLYUNKNOWNStrain-specific toxin protoxins that crystallize into the parasporal inclusion and can become active against susceptible cells after solubilization and proteolytic activation.
  • DOI:10.3390/toxins6123296 Palma et al. 2014 review Bacillus thuringiensis parasporal crystalline inclusions as containing Cry and Cyt proteins, plus additional crystal proteins of varied known or unknown activities.
  • DOI:10.3390/toxins6041222 Ben-Dov 2014 reviews the Bacillus thuringiensis serovar israelensis toxin plasmid and summarizes Cry4Aa, Cry4Ba, Cry11Aa, Cyt1Aa, Cry10Aa, and Cyt2Ba as the polypeptides that form its complex parasporal crystalline body.

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:1428 Bacillus thuringiensis — VARIABLE: Parasporal crystalline inclusions are characteristic of many Bacillus thuringiensis isolates, but crystal protein complements are plasmid-associated and vary among strains. [DOI:10.3390/toxins6123296]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:1430 Bacillus thuringiensis serovar israelensis — Serovar with a complex ovoid parasporal inclusion whose electron-microscopic lattice segments and toxin polypeptide classes were resolved biochemically. [DOI:10.1128/aem.50.1.56-62.1985]

Functions

  • pesticidal protein packaging — Parasporal crystals package Cry, Cyt, and related pesticidal protoxins as insoluble crystalline deposits.
    • DOI:10.3390/toxins6123296 Palma et al. 2014 review Cry/Cyt-containing parasporal inclusions and the downstream biocidal activities of Bt crystal proteins.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.toxicon.2006.11.022 Bravo, Gill and Soberon 2007 review Cry and Cyt toxins as Bacillus thuringiensis protein families that form pores in target membranes after activation.
