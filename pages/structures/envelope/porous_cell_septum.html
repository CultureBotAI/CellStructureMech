porous cell septum

GO:0000934 · Envelope · Envelope layer · Proposed

A septum or cross wall which does not entirely span the space between two portions of cell wall and may contain a specialized central pore structure. A porous septum allows the movement of organelles and/or cytoplasm between compartments. A cross-wall cell septum in filamentous ascomycete and basidiomycete fungi that is perforated by one or more central septal pores (such as the simple septal pore of Neurospora crassa and Aspergillus nidulans or the barrel-shaped dolipore septum of basidiomycetes). The porous cell septum partitions hyphal compartments structurally while permitting regulated intercellular cytoplasmic streaming, organelle migration, and Woronin-body- or parenthesome-mediated pore gating upon hyphal wounding. [GO:0000934]

Synonyms: porous septum

  • NCBITaxon:5334 Schizophyllum commune — Wood-rot agaricomycete model in which septal pore cap composition and its requirement for normal septal closure, vegetative growth and mushroom formation were genetically tested. [DOI:10.1111/j.1462-2920.2009.02122.x]

Functions

  • septal pore gatekeeping — The dolipore septum and its paired septal pore caps regulate cytoplasmic continuity between adjacent hyphal compartments by controlling whether the central septal pore is open or closed.
    • DOI:10.1128/EC.00125-08 Van Driel et al. 2008 found SPC18 in septal pore caps and pore plugs, supporting septal pore cap participation in dolipore plugging.
    • DOI:10.1111/j.1462-2920.2009.02122.x Van Peer et al. 2010 linked Schizophyllum commune septal pore cap loss to an inability to close septa.
