plasma membrane

GO:0005886 · ENVELOPE · MEMBRANE · PROPOSED

The lipid membrane surrounding a cell that separates the cytoplasm from the external environment and houses proteins for selective transport, signaling, and envelope biogenesis. [GO:0005886]

Synonyms: cell membrane; cytoplasmic membrane; bacterial inner membrane

Part of: GO:0071944

Functions

  • selective permeability barrier — The plasma membrane separates cytoplasm from the external milieu while membrane proteins mediate controlled import, export, and ion movement.
    • DOI:10.1101/cshperspect.a000414 Silhavy, Kahne and Walker 2010 review transport across the bacterial inner membrane as a central envelope activity.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro2576 Albers and Meyer 2011 review the archaeal cell envelope as bounded by a cytoplasmic membrane built from ether lipids.
    • DOI:10.1146/annurev-micro-091313-103507 Douglas and Konopka 2014 review fungal plasma-membrane compartmentation of transporters and other integral membrane proteins.
  • membrane-embedded bioenergetics — In bacteria and archaea, respiratory and ATP-synthase complexes in the cytoplasmic membrane use ion gradients across the membrane for energy conservation.
    • DOI:10.1101/cshperspect.a000414 Silhavy, Kahne and Walker 2010 describe the bacterial cytoplasmic membrane as the energized membrane that supports transport and envelope biogenesis.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro2576 Albers and Meyer 2011 review archaeal cytoplasmic membranes as energy-transducing envelope membranes.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Use the GO label plasma membrane while preserving cytoplasmic membrane as the microbial envelope name. GO:0005886 is the exact cellular-component term. Bacterial and archaeal literature usually calls the same cell-bounding structure the cytoplasmic membrane or, in Gram-negative bacteria, the inner membrane.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Avoid bilayer-only wording for the taxon-spanning plasma membrane record. Bacterial and fungal plasma membranes are bilayers built largely from ester-linked lipids, whereas archaeal cytoplasmic membranes use ether lipids and can be diether bilayers or tetraether monolayers. This broad record therefore uses lipid-boundary wording and leaves exact lipid topology to lineage-specific records.
