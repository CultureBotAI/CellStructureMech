S-layer

GO:0030115 · ENVELOPE · ENVELOPE_LAYER · PROPOSED

A paracrystalline monomolecular array of one or more (glyco)protein species that self-assemble on the outermost surface of the cell envelope, with oblique, square or hexagonal lattice symmetry. S-layers are common in Archaea, where they are often the sole wall component, and widespread but patchily distributed in Bacteria. [DOI:10.1111/1574-6976.12063]

Synonyms: surface layer; crystalline cell surface layer

Functions

  • cell shape maintenance and envelope stabilisation — Contributes to shape and osmotic stability, especially in Archaea lacking a rigid wall.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro2576 Albers & Meyer 2011.
  • molecular sieving and surface interaction — Lattice pores set a size cut-off; the outer face mediates adhesion and phage or immune interactions.
    • DOI:10.1038/nrmicro3213 Fagan & Fairweather 2014.

Mechanism graphs

S-layer proteins self-assemble into a surface lattice (ASSEMBLY)

Secreted S-layer proteins self-assemble into a crystalline two-dimensional lattice that coats the outer face of the cell. In many Gram-positive S-layers, SLH domains within the lattice-forming protein bind secondary cell-wall polymers to retain the lattice on the peptidoglycan-linked envelope surface.
