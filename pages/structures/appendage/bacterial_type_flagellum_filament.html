bacterial-type flagellum filament

GO:0009420 · APPENDAGE · FILAMENT · PROPOSED

The long (approximately 20 nm), thin external structure of the bacterial-type flagellum, which acts as a propeller. [GO:0009420]

Synonyms: flagellar filament; flagellin-based flagellum filament

Part of: GO:0009288

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — COMMON: External flagellin filaments are widespread among bacterial-type flagella but are absent from non-flagellated bacterial lineages and from periplasmic spirochete variants whose filaments sit inside the outer membrane. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1887]
  • NCBITaxon:2157 Archaea — ABSENT: Archaea swim with archaella, which are type-IV-pilus-related appendages rather than homologs of bacterial flagellin filaments. [DOI:10.3389/fmicb.2015.00023]

Functions

  • helical propeller thrust — Motor-driven rotation of the helical filament generates thrust for swimming motility.
    • DOI:10.1146/annurev.biochem.72.121801.161737 Berg 2003 reviews how rotation of a helical bacterial flagellar filament propels swimming cells.

Mechanism graphs

Flagellin protofilaments form a rotary propeller (FUNCTION)

Flagellin subunits pack into protofilaments that form a long tubular helix. When the basal body transmits torque through the hook, rotation of that helical filament generates thrust for swimming motility.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should the hook, hook-filament junction, or FliD cap be curated as bacterial-type flagellum filament components? The filament record is scoped to the flagellin polymer. FlgE, FlgK/FlgL, and FliD build adjoining or terminal axial substructures: the hook and hook-filament junction are separate flagellar substructures, and FliD forms the GO:0009421 filament-cap substructure of the enclosing bacterial-type flagellum rather than a flagellin-filament component.
