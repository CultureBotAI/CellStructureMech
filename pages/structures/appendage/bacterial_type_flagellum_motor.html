bacterial-type flagellum motor

GO:0120100 · APPENDAGE · MULTIPROTEIN_COMPLEX · PROPOSED

A bacterial flagellar basal-body motor composed of an MS-ring/C-ring rotor complex and one or more Mot/Pom-family stator complexes in the cytoplasmic membrane that convert ion flow into rotary torque and bias clockwise or counterclockwise output. [DOI:10.1016/j.tim.2014.12.011]

Synonyms: bacterial flagellar motor; flagellar motor

Part of: GO:0009425

Has part: GO:0120107 GO:0120101

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — VARIABLE: Bacterial-type flagellum motors occur in bacteria with rotary bacterial flagella and are absent from bacteria that lack the flagellar system. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1887]
  • NCBITaxon:2157 Archaea — ABSENT: Archaeal archaella are type-IV-pilus-related rotary appendages and do not contain FliF/FliG/FliM/FliN rotors or Mot/Pom stators. [DOI:10.3389/fmicb.2015.00023]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:90371 Salmonella enterica subsp. enterica serovar Typhimurium — Source organism for clockwise-locked C-ring electron microscopy and a FliG/FliM/FliN rotor-architecture model. [DOI:10.1128/JB.00552-06]

Functions

  • ion-driven rotary torque — Proton- or sodium-driven stator units apply torque to the C-ring face of the rotor to spin the MS ring and axial rod.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.tim.2014.12.011 Minamino and Imada 2015 review the rotor-stator interface used for bacterial flagellar torque.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2020.08.016 Santiveri et al. 2020 modeled MotAB stator rotation as the force source for flagellar torque generation.
  • dynamic stator anchoring — Stator units dock around the rotor and anchor through their B-subunit peptidoglycan-binding domains while dynamically exchanging at the motor.
    • DOI:10.7554/eLife.48979 Chang et al. 2019 imaged stator complexes around bacterial flagellar rotors in cells.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.str.2018.02.016 Kojima et al. 2018 linked B-subunit rearrangement to peptidoglycan binding and stator activation.
  • chemotactic directional switching — Phosphorylated CheY binds FliM in the C-ring switch complex to shift the motor between clockwise and counterclockwise rotational states.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.90.19.8787 Welch et al. 1993 directly demonstrated phosphorylation-dependent CheY binding to FliM in the switch complex.
    • DOI:10.1038/nature09300 Lee et al. 2010 connected the FliG/FliM/FliN torque ring to rotational switching.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should the GO definition's A4B2 stator stoichiometry be copied? GO:0120100 remains the exact cellular-component term for the bacterial flagellar motor, but its 2017 definition predates the 2020 MotAB structures that resolved the stator core as A5B2 rather than A4B2.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should the rod, hook, filament, LP rings, or export apparatus be curated as motor components? The bacterial flagellar motor is the membrane rotor plus stator torque generator. The rod, hook and filament transmit or receive the motor output, the LP rings act as envelope bushings, and the export apparatus secretes axial subunits, but they are outside the rotor/stator motor boundary.
