archaeal cannula

cellstructuremech:archaeal_cannula · APPENDAGE · APPENDAGE · PROPOSED

An extracellular hollow tubular archaeal cell projection, characterized in Pyrodictium species, that extends from the cell envelope as a proteinaceous filament and forms networks between cells. [DOI:10.1016/S1047-8477(02)00581-6]

Synonyms: cannulae

CanA cannula glycoproteinPROTEINpart ofREVIEWED_LABEL_ONLYUNKNOWNExtracellular glycoprotein subunit that polymerizes into hollow archaeal cannula fibers in a calcium-dependent, chaperone-free reaction.
  • DOI:10.1038/s41467-025-64120-8 Sleutel et al. 2025 used cryo-EM on ex vivo and in vitro Pyrodictium abyssi CanA fibers to resolve calcium-coordinated, donor-strand-complemented cannula polymerization.
  • DOI:10.1038/s41598-025-13242-6 Munte et al. 2025 solved the CanA solution structure and characterized divalent-ion-induced CanA polymerization.

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:54256 Pyrodictium abyssi — COMMON: Pyrodictium abyssi produces extracellular cannulae that were visualized as a cell-associated tubular network in ultrastructural studies. [DOI:10.1006/jsbi.1995.1032]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:54256 Pyrodictium abyssi — Hyperthermophilic archaeon in which cannulae were shown by cryo-electron tomography to enter the periplasmic space without crossing into the cytoplasm. [DOI:10.1016/S1047-8477(02)00581-6]

Functions

  • extracellular Pyrodictium cell-network formation — Cannulae extend from the Pyrodictium cell envelope and physically interconnect cells in an extracellular network; the transported cargo or signaling role of the network remains unresolved.
    • DOI:10.1016/S1047-8477(02)00581-6 Nickell et al. 2003 used cryo-electron tomography to show how Pyrodictium cannulae contact the cell envelope and interconnect cells.
    • DOI:10.1006/jsbi.1995.1032 Rieger et al. 1995 characterized the extracellular network of Pyrodictium abyssi cannulae in ultrastructural sections.
