bacterial-type flagellum stator complex

GO:0120101 · Appendage · Multiprotein complex · Proposed

An ion-driven membrane complex in the bacterial flagellar motor, typically with five A subunits surrounding two B subunits, that transduces proton or sodium flow into torque on the flagellar rotor and anchors to the cell wall through the B subunits. [DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2020.08.016]

Synonyms: bacterial-type flagellum motor force generator complex; bacterial-type flagellum torque generator complex

Part of: bacterial-type flagellum motor (GO:0120100)

Taxonomic distribution

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — VARIABLE: MotA/MotB- and PomA/PomB-family stator complexes occur in rotary bacterial flagellar motors but are absent from bacteria without bacterial flagella. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1887]
  • NCBITaxon:2157 Archaea — ABSENT: Archaeal archaella are type-IV-pilus-related appendages and do not use Mot/Pom stator complexes for rotation. [DOI:10.3389/fmicb.2015.00023]

Canonical examples

  • NCBITaxon:197 Campylobacter jejuni — Source genus and species for one of the high-resolution MotA5MotB2 stator structures that revised the core stoichiometry. [DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2020.08.016]

Functions

  • ion-driven torque generation — Ion flow powers stator torque on the flagellar rotor. The 2020 structural models propose A-pentamer motion around the B dimer; direct polarized-photobleaching measurements subsequently supported MotA rotation in Escherichia coli, not every Mot/Pom stator.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.cell.2020.08.016 Santiveri et al. 2020 combined MotAB structures, mutagenesis and functional assays into a model for stator rotation and torque generation.
    • DOI:10.1038/s41564-020-0788-8 Deme et al. 2020 modeled MotA5MotB2 as the ion-driven gear that drives the FliG rotor ring.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.2515291122 Hosu et al. 2025 measured MotA rotation relative to anchored MotB in functioning Escherichia coli motors using polarized photobleaching. The experiment measured angular speed, not the direction of MotA rotation, and did not test all Mot/Pom stators.
  • dynamic peptidoglycan anchoring — The B-subunit periplasmic domain binds peptidoglycan after activation. Exchange of motor-bound stators with a membrane pool has been measured in Escherichia coli; this turnover behavior is not assumed for every scaffolded bacterial motor.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.str.2018.02.016 Kojima et al. 2018 linked B-subunit periplasmic-domain rearrangement to peptidoglycan binding and stator activation.
    • DOI:10.1038/nature05135 Leake et al. 2006 measured GFP-MotB turnover between the membrane pool and functioning Escherichia coli motors using fluorescence recovery and loss after photobleaching; this is direct exchange evidence for that system, not every bacterial motor.

Physical properties

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should GO:0120101's historical A4B2 stator stoichiometry be copied into this record? GO:0120101 is still the exact cellular-component identifier for the bacterial flagellar stator complex, but its text was minted before high-resolution MotAB structures revised the core from A4B2 to A5B2.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should the C ring, MS ring, FliL, MotX/MotY, or other stator-associated scaffolds be curated as core stator components? The mature GO:0120101 stator complex is scoped to the ion-conducting A/B subunit core, including proton-driven MotA/MotB and sodium-driven PomA/PomB variants. The MS ring and C ring are rotor-side structures that the stator acts on, while FliL and lineage-specific MotX/MotY-family scaffolds decorate or recruit stators outside the conserved A/B core.
