periplasmic flagellum

GO:0055040 · Appendage · Appendage · Proposed

A bacterial-type flagellar appendage of spirochetes whose filaments reside in the periplasmic space between the cytoplasmic and outer membranes, attach near the cell poles, and rotate to deform the cell cylinder for motility. [DOI:10.3390/biom10040550]

Synonyms: endoflagellum; periplasmic flagellar filament

  • NCBITaxon:203691 Spirochaetota — COMMON: Periplasmic flagella are characteristic endoflagella of motile spirochetes and reside between the inner and outer membranes. [DOI:10.3390/biom10040550]

  • NCBITaxon:139 Borreliella burgdorferi — Qin et al. imaged intact periplasmic flagella and basal-body motors in Borrelia burgdorferi cells by cryo-electron tomography. [DOI:10.1371/journal.pbio.3000050]

Functions

  • bacterial-type flagellum-dependent cell motility GO:0071973 — Internal flagellar rotation within the periplasm deforms the spirochete cell cylinder and produces swimming motility.
    • DOI:10.3390/biom10040550 Nakamura 2020 reviews propulsion by rotation of periplasmic flagella inside the narrow spirochete periplasmic space.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.200221797 Motaleb et al. 2000 found flaB mutants nonmotile and rod-shaped, but CCCP-immobilized wild-type cells retained flat-wave shape. The skeletal shape contribution therefore does not require rotation and is distinct from dynamic deformation during swimming.

Periplasmic flagellar rotation drives dynamic cell-body deformation during swimming (FUNCTION)

FlaB builds the periplasmic filament; FlaA association and sheath architecture differ across spirochete lineages. The polar basal body drives filament rotation and dynamic cell-body deformation during swimming. This motility pathway is distinct from the rotation-independent skeletal contribution to resting Borrelia shape.
