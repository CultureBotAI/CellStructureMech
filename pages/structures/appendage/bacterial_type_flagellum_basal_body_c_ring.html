bacterial-type flagellum basal body, C ring

GO:0009433 · APPENDAGE · MULTIPROTEIN_COMPLEX · PROPOSED

The cytoplasmic FliG/FliM/FliN switch ring of the bacterial flagellar basal body, assembled on the MS ring to form the rotor face that receives torque from Mot/Pom stator units and changes rotational bias in response to phosphorylated CheY. [DOI:10.1016/j.tim.2014.12.011]

Synonyms: flagellar basal body, C ring; flagellin-based flagellum basal body, C ring; flagellar C ring; flagellar switch complex

Part of: GO:0009425

  • NCBITaxon:2 Bacteria — VARIABLE: FliG/FliM/FliN-family C rings occur in bacteria that assemble bacterial-type flagellar basal bodies and are absent from lineages that lack this rotary appendage. [DOI:10.1038/nrmicro1887]
  • NCBITaxon:2157 Archaea — ABSENT: Archaea assemble type-IV-pilus-related archaella rather than FliG/FliM/FliN bacterial flagellar C rings. [DOI:10.3389/fmicb.2015.00023]

Functions

  • basal-body rotor assembly — FliG attaches to the cytoplasmic side of the FliF MS ring and recruits the FliM/FliN switch lattice to build the mature C-ring rotor.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.89.14.6304 Francis et al. 1992 placed FliG at the cytoplasmic face of the Salmonella M ring.
    • DOI:10.1038/s41467-021-24507-9 Kawamoto et al. 2021 measured native Salmonella C-ring symmetry relative to the FliF MS-ring core.
  • rotor-stator torque coupling — FliG in the C ring contacts membrane stator units so Mot/Pom A-subunit rotation can apply torque to the basal-body rotor.
    • DOI:10.1016/j.tim.2014.12.011 Minamino and Imada 2015 review the FliG-stator interface that transmits motor torque to the C ring.
    • DOI:10.1038/s41564-020-0788-8 Deme et al. 2020 modeled MotA/PomA-family stator subunits meshing with FliG in the rotor.
  • rotational switching — The switch complex changes C-ring conformation to bias the flagellar motor between clockwise and counterclockwise rotation after phosphorylated CheY binds primarily to FliM.
    • DOI:10.1073/pnas.90.19.8787 Welch et al. 1993 showed that CheY binds the switch complex with FliM as the primary docking site and that the extent of CheY-FliM binding depends on CheY phosphorylation.
    • DOI:10.1038/nature09300 Lee et al. 2010 connected FliG/FliM/FliN structure to the molecular basis of flagellar rotational switching.
    • DOI:10.1128/mBio.00079-19 Sakai et al. 2019 experimentally examined conformational rearrangements within the switch complex.

Discussions

  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should Bacillus subtilis FliY be folded into the curated FliN component? Ward et al. 2019 support FliY as the Bacillus subtilis lower switch-complex protein, but the exact InterPro grounding used for this record's FliN component is restricted to FliN from organisms such as Salmonella and Escherichia.
  • CURATION_TODO (RESOLVED): Should the MS ring, stator units or flagellar export apparatus be components of this record? GO:0009433 names only the cytoplasmic C ring. The FliF MS ring, Mot/Pom stator complexes and type III export apparatus are neighboring basal-body substructures that physically and functionally couple to the C ring but are not its FliG/FliM/FliN constituents.
